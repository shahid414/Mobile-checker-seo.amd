What is robots.txt?

A robots.txt file tells search engine crawlers which parts of a site they may visit. It sits at the root of a domain, for example example.com/robots.txt, and uses User-agent groups with Allow and Disallow rules, plus optional Sitemap lines.

A single wrong line — Disallow: / — can remove a whole site from Google. That is why this tester flags it immediately when Googlebot is blocked, and why you should test robots.txt after every change.

Frequently asked questions

What if a site has no robots.txt?

That is fine — search engines will crawl the whole site. The tester will tell you no file was found.

Does robots.txt hide pages from Google?

No — it only asks crawlers not to visit them. To keep a page out of search results, use a noindex tag instead.

What is the Sitemap line for?

It points crawlers to your sitemap.xml so they can discover all your pages faster.
