What is mobile-first indexing?

Mobile-first indexing means Google uses the mobile version of a page's content for indexing and ranking. Google rolled it out over several years and announced in October 2023 that the move was complete. Since July 2024 it crawls remaining sites with Googlebot Smartphone only.

That is why parity matters: a paragraph, heading or piece of structured data that exists on desktop but not on mobile no longer exists for Google. The risk lies in dynamic serving, separate mobile URLs and themes that trim content for phones. With responsive design these conditions usually hold on their own, because both devices get the same HTML.

Frequently asked questions

What does this checker compare?

It fetches the same URL as Googlebot Smartphone and Googlebot Desktop, then compares 14 signals: status, host, title, description, H1, canonical, robots tag, viewport, word count, links, images and structured data.

What is a good parity score?

90 or above means mobile and desktop match well. Below 70 means a lot differs — content missing on mobile is invisible to Google, so fix the differing rows first.

Does it use real Googlebot?

No — it is an independent check that fetches with Googlebot user-agent strings. To see what Google itself indexed, use Search Console's URL Inspection tool.
