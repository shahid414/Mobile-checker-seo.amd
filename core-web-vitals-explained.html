Core Web Vitals Explained — LCP, INP & CLS

Core Web Vitals are the three metrics Google uses to judge how a page feels: how fast the main content shows up (LCP), how quickly it responds to a tap (INP), and whether the layout jumps while loading (CLS).

LCP — Largest Contentful Paint

LCP measures how long the biggest visible element — usually a hero image or a headline — takes to appear. Good is 2.5 seconds or less, over 4 seconds is poor. Google measures it at the 75th percentile of real users, so a fast phone on good wifi does not hide a slow experience elsewhere.

Fix it by compressing the largest image and serving it at the right size, preloading it, and removing render-blocking scripts from the head. On most sites the hero image is the whole story.

INP — Interaction to Next Paint

INP measures how long a page takes to respond after a tap or click — opening a menu, pressing a button, typing in a field. Good is 200 milliseconds or less, over 500 ms is poor. It replaced First Input Delay in March 2024.

Fix it by breaking up long JavaScript tasks, deferring third-party scripts, and avoiding heavy work on the main thread. In lab tests Lighthouse shows Total Blocking Time instead, because nobody taps anything in a lab run.

CLS — Cumulative Layout Shift

CLS measures how much the layout jumps while a page loads — text moving down when an image arrives, a button shifting as a font loads. Good is 0.1 or less, over 0.25 is poor. It is the only Core Web Vital with no time unit: it is a score, not a duration.

Fix it by giving every image and video explicit width and height, reserving space for ads and embeds, and never inserting content above what the visitor is already reading.

How to check your Core Web Vitals

Run our website speed test for a lab measurement with the exact numbers, or open Search Console → Core Web Vitals → Mobile for real-user data across your whole site. When lab and field disagree, trust the field data — that is the experience Google evaluates.

Check mobile separately from desktop: the mobile field data is what applies to mobile searches, which is where most traffic usually is.

Frequently asked questions

Do Core Web Vitals affect rankings?

Yes — they are part of Google's ranking systems. But good scores do not guarantee top positions; the most relevant content can still rank with weak speed.

What is a good LCP?

2.5 seconds or less at the 75th percentile. Between 2.5 and 4 seconds needs improvement; over 4 seconds is poor.

Why does my layout shift?

Usually images, ads or embeds without reserved space. Give every image width and height and reserve space for anything that loads late.
